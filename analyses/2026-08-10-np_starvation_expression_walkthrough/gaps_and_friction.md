**2026-08-10 — `growth_phase` doesn't always identify the "starvation" timepoint, and one experiment's stated significance criterion doesn't match its available data.** While selecting a single representative starvation timepoint per experiment (step 5), two gaps surfaced: (1) Tolonen et al. 2006's rapid-onset nitrogen-deprivation time courses (MED4 and MIT9313 microarray) never reach `growth_phase = nutrient_limited` at any of their 6 timepoints (0-48h) — every timepoint is labeled `acute_stress`, so the KG alone gives no signal for "when did starvation take hold." Resolved with researcher-supplied knowledge from the source paper (cultures began declining at 12h), not a KG field — tagged `[user-provided]` in `5_analyze/notebook.md`. (2) Martiny et al. 2006's MIT9313 phosphorus microarray experiment carries the description "genes with q<0.05 at t=48h" (copied from the same paper's MED4 entry), but the KG's actual timepoint data for MIT9313 stops at 24h — no 48h row exists. Cannot determine from the KG whether this is a missing-data gap or whether the description was never updated for MIT9313. Included in the analysis with a visible caveat rather than resolved. Impact: any future work with `growth_phase` should not assume it marks "starvation achieved" without checking whether the experiment is a rapid-onset design; any use of a `table_scope_detail` string should be verified against the experiment's actual timepoint list before trusting it strain-by-strain within a multi-strain publication.

**2026-08-12 — The nitrogen "background/noise rate" is not one number; it
ranges from 3.0% to 47.2% across the 4 background experiments.** While
evaluating H3 in step 6, breaking the pooled 18.8% background mean out
per experiment showed a ~16-fold spread (Tolonen MIT9313 microarray 3.0%,
Weissberg proteomics 9.1%, Tolonen MED4 microarray 17.6%, Weissberg
RNA-seq 47.2% — this last one higher than the observed matched-nitrogen
target rate itself, 41.3%). The bootstrap in `4_methods/np_response.py`
already handles this correctly (it draws each iteration's random sample
from the same per-experiment pools the real target genes were tested in,
not from one pooled rate), so the H3 result is not compromised — but the
single pooled mean reported in step 5's notebook understates how much the
"noise" floor varies by platform/experiment. Impact: future analyses
using this bootstrap pattern should report (or at least check) the
per-experiment background rate, not just the pooled mean/std, since a
single "background rate" can be misleading on its own even when the
underlying resampling is done correctly.

**2026-08-10 — MIT9312 phosphorus evidence collapses under `significant_only` table_scope.** Step 2 resolved 44 of the researcher's 92 genes to a locus tag in MIT9312, but only 2 have actual DE evidence. MIT9312's sole in-scope experiment (Fuszard et al. 2012 proteomics, `10.1186/2046-9063-8-7`) has `table_scope = significant_only` — the source publication's supplementary table only lists genes that passed its own fold-change cutoff (>1.6 or <0.6), so every gene not already significant in that paper is invisible to this KG build, not "tested and not significant." **2026-09-06 — Post-closure verification, live KG: quantified exactly how narrow every phosphorus experiment's gene coverage is, and confirmed two different "missing gene" mechanisms.** The researcher asked (after the analysis was already closed) why specific target genes show "no data" in Figure 1, and whether phosphorus's low coverage was really a property of the source tables or a KG gap. Re-ran queries against the live KG (down for part of this session; confirmed working 2026-09-06) rather than trusting the closed analysis's own summary text, and found two distinct mechanisms. Scripts and outputs committed to `6_evaluate/scripts/03_post_closure_missing_gene_checks.py` and `04_post_closure_phosphorus_coverage.py` (data: `6_evaluate/data/03_post_closure_missing_gene_checks.txt`, `03_read_target_gene_presence.csv`, `04_phosphorus_genome_coverage.csv`).
- **Declared publication-level filter** (Read et al. 2017 MED4 RNA-seq, nitrogen): confirmed empirically that its `table_scope_detail` ("Top 50% of genes by expression level") is applied genome-wide, not per-timepoint — three example genes (cynD, pipX, urtD; all present and evidenced elsewhere in MED4) are absent from **all 3** of the experiment's timepoints (3h, 12h, 24h), not just the 24h timepoint step 5 chose as the representative starvation point. A gene missing from Read's table at one timepoint is missing from all of them.
- **Platform/probe coverage gap, no declared filter** (phoE / locus `PMT_2631` in MIT9313): this locus has **zero DE rows in any MIT9313 experiment in the entire KG** (checked across all 6 Tolonen timepoints and across every MIT9313 experiment regardless of nutrient/omics type) — unlike Read, Tolonen's MIT9313 table is a broad, largely unfiltered sample (76% of the genome; see below), so this looks like a per-locus platform gap (e.g. no probe on the 2006 microarray) rather than a significance-based exclusion. Aside: the KG's own `gene_name` field for this Cyanorak ortholog group (`CK_00002330`) is `som`/unset in all 4 strains, not literally "phoE" — all four are annotated `product = "outer membrane porin"`, consistent with phoE's known function, so this reads as a real ortholog match with an inconsistent name field, not a resolution error.
- **Quantified genome coverage for all 5 phosphorus experiments** (distinct genes in each KG table vs. that strain's total `gene_count`): Lin RNA-seq (NATL2A) 34/2,214 = 1.5%; Fuszard proteomics (MIT9312) 38/1,978 = 1.9%; Fuszard proteomics (NATL2A) 63/2,214 = 2.8%; Martiny microarray (MED4) 34/1,976 = 1.7%; Martiny microarray (MIT9313) 177/2,948 = 6.0%. All 5 land in a narrow 1.5-6.0% band, regardless of method (RNA-seq, proteomics, microarray) — confirming this is a property of each source publication's own filtered supplementary table, not a limitation of the underlying technology: on the nitrogen side, the microarray tables (Tolonen) cover 76-86% of the genome using the same platform type. Proteomics (the 2 Fuszard tables) additionally has some intrinsic coverage limitation beyond the publication filter (peptide-based detection covers less of the genome than RNA-seq/microarray even before any significance cutoff).

Impact: confirms and quantifies (rather than changes) step 3's decision to exclude phosphorus from the H3 background/noise check — none of the 5 phosphorus experiments has an unfiltered "everything tested" table, by a wide margin (max 6.0% of genome vs. nitrogen's 76%+). Also gives a concrete diagnostic for future "why is gene X missing" questions: check the experiment's `table_scope`/`table_scope_detail` first (declared filter → check all timepoints, not just the chosen one, since filters are typically study-wide) before assuming a platform/probe coverage gap (which shows up as zero rows for that locus across every experiment for the strain, not just one study).

**2026-09-08 — The Cyanorak ortholog-group ID is not reliably 1:1 with a gene; `02_resolve_genes.py` silently kept the wrong paralog.** Tracing a Figure 1 cell, the researcher found `phoE` reading as "not significant" in Lin et al. 2015 when the KG has a strongly P-induced porin in the NATL2A pho island. Root cause: `phoE`'s Cyanorak ID `CK_00002330` is an **"outer membrane porin" family with 3–6 members per strain**, and `02_resolve_genes.py` built `{organism_name: locus_tag}` — a dict keyed by strain — so when a group had several members the last one silently overwrote the rest. The kept locus was a non-pho-island porin in MED4, MIT9312, NATL2A. (This directly overturns the 2026-09-06 entry's conclusion that `CK_00002330` was "a real ortholog match with an inconsistent name field, not a resolution error" — it *was* a resolution error; the 2026-09-06 check looked at the wrong locus (`PMT_2631`) without noticing the group had 5 members.)
- **Code fix:** resolve to a list of members per strain, not a dict; 1 member → resolve, multi-member → curated `MANUAL_LOCUS_OVERRIDE` (by synteny) or flag `ambiguous_multi_member` and leave unresolved. Never guess.
- **Systematic check 1 — `2_kg_selection/scripts/05_paralog_audit.py`:** for every one of the 91 Cyanorak IDs, count `genes_by_homolog_group` members per in-scope strain. >1 = the "pick one" logic was guessing. Found 4 (`phoE`, `unkP2`, `pstS`-MIT9313, `PMM719`-NATL2A). This is a pure structural check — no biology needed.
- **Systematic check 2 — `2_kg_selection/scripts/06_name_vs_cyanorak_crosscheck.py`:** the resolution pipeline keys *only* on the Cyanorak ID, so an independent second identifier is needed. Resolve each gene by *name* (plus normalised ORF forms) via `resolve_gene`, diff against the Cyanorak route. `DISAGREE` / `name_only` are the flags. Found 2 (see next entry).
- **Per-hit confirmation:** `gene_overview` (what the locus is) → `gene_homologs` (which group it really belongs to) → `gene_neighbors` (synteny: the pho regulon is one physical cluster) → `differential_expression_by_gene` (does the chosen locus behave like the gene should).

Impact: 6 gene loci corrected (4× `phoE`, `unkP2`, `urtA`; plus `ptrA`-NATL2A added). Cascaded through steps 5–6. The two audit scripts + their frozen CSVs should be a standard step-2 QC for any analysis that resolves a supplied gene list through Cyanorak IDs — the multi-member problem is invisible without check 1, and a wrong ID in the source spreadsheet is invisible without check 2. Retrospective methodology gap: the original step-2 QC gate only checked "does the ID match *a* group," never "does it match *one* gene."

**2026-09-08 — Two genes in the researcher's source spreadsheet carry the wrong Cyanorak ID.** Found by `06_name_vs_cyanorak_crosscheck.py` (name route vs ID route).
- **`urtA`** was given `CK_00008074` — which is **`urtE`'s** ortholog group ("ABC-type urea transporter, ATPase component"). Both the `urtA` and `urtE` spreadsheet rows carried that ID, so both resolved to `urtE`'s locus (`PMM0974` in MED4 etc.). The analysis had `urtE` twice and no `urtA` at all — and the step-5 notebook's earlier "urtA/urtE resolve to an identical locus, a source-list data-quality issue, show both" note was treating the symptom as unfixable when the fix was to use the correct `urtA` group `CK_00000076` (`PMM0970`, "substrate-binding component"). Real `urtA` is more N-responsive than `urtE`, so H1 nitrogen matched rate rose 41.3% → 44.2%.
- **`ptrA`** was given `CK_00056804`, a MED4-only singleton Cyanorak group (1 member, `PMM0718`). NATL2A's `ptrA` — locus `PMN2A_0435`, literally named `ptrA` in the KG, in the pho island next to `phoB`, in Lin et al.'s reported gene set — is in a different group (`CK_00001606`) and was flagged "not in genome." MIT9312/MIT9313 genuinely lack the pho-island Crp regulator (checked by synteny — no Crp gene in either pho island).

Fix: `CYANORAK_ID_OVERRIDE` (swap `urtA`'s ID before resolution) and `FORCE_LOCUS` (patch `ptrA`-NATL2A) in `02_resolve_genes.py`, each documented inline. Impact: confirms that trusting a supplied gene list's identifiers without an independent cross-check is a real risk, not a theoretical one — 2 of 91 IDs were wrong. Neither was catchable by the multi-member audit (check 1); only the name cross-check (check 2) found them.

**2026-09-08 — Lin et al. 2015: the representative starvation timepoint was misread (46h chosen; 59h is the right one).** Step 5 chose 46h for the Lin NATL2A phosphorus experiment on the rationale "last `nutrient_limited` timepoint before phosphate was added back." That misread the two-arm design: the KG's timepoint list is `4h, 24h, 46h` (`nutrient_limited`), `50h (P added)` (`recovery`), **`59h` (`nutrient_limited`)**, `59h (P added)` (`recovery`). The plain `59h` is a *later* starvation point in the arm that never got phosphate — the `(P added)` suffix marks the recovery arm, which was correctly excluded. At 46h only 4 of Lin's 34 genes clear significance; at 59h it is 18 — the `pst` operon and `phoR` do not fully engage until 59h, so 46h substantially under-represented the response Lin reported. Verified the KG's `expression_status` is a genuine per-timepoint DESeq2 call (e.g. `pstA` 46h padj=1.0, 59h padj=4e-5), not a study-level flag. Fix: `STARVATION_TIMEPOINT` in `5_analyze/scripts/01_extract_target_de.py` → `59h`, researcher-directed. Impact: any future use of a multi-arm nutrient time course must check whether a same-hour timepoint pair (`59h` vs `59h (P added)`) is two arms, not one; `growth_phase` distinguishes them but the bare timepoint label does not.

**2026-09-08 — Martiny et al. 2006: "gene absent from the filtered table" reclassified as "tested, not significant" (Martiny only).** The 2 Martiny phosphorus microarray experiments have `table_scope = filtered_subset` with the filter "genes with q<0.05 at t=48h" — a pure significance filter on a genome-wide microarray. So a target gene with a locus in the strain but no row in the Martiny table was measured on the array and did not pass, i.e. `not_significant`, not "no data." Reclassified for those 2 experiments only in `5_analyze/scripts/01_extract_target_de.py` (`RECLASSIFY_ABSENT_AS_NOT_SIG`). **Not** applied to Fuszard (iTRAQ proteomics — checked the live KG: it holds a fixed 38/63-protein list per experiment; a gene outside that list was not detected as a peptide, genuinely "no data") or Lin (curated operon-level table — its 34 genes include several never significant at any timepoint, so it is not a threshold-defined set). Impact: this is the `significant_only` / `filtered_subset` ambiguity the KG conventions doc flags ("tested-absent collapses with not-detected — you can't tell from the KG"); the reclassification is defensible for a genome-wide microarray with a pure significance filter, and only there. Phosphorus matched hit rate: the reclassification lowered it (fewer inflated all-significant denominators), the `phoE` fix + Lin 59h raised it — net 75.7% → 60.6%.
